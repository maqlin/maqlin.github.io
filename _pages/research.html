I study how galaxies and dark matter haloes assemble inside the cosmic web, and how their clustering constrains cosmology. The work falls into three directions.

  1. Galaxy formation and evolution
  2. Dark matter haloes within large-scale structure
  3. Cosmology

Galaxy formation and evolution

How halo conditions, together with baryonic physics, set the structure of galaxies, from dwarf galaxies to the most massive systems across cosmic time.

Halo spin bias and ultra-diffuse galaxies

arXiv ADS Publisher (accepted by MNRAS)

Isolated blue ultra-diffuse galaxies in SDSS cluster as strongly as massive groups, which standard subhalo models do not reproduce. In TNG300-Dark the discrepancy depends on the definition of halo spin: spin from bound particles alone fails, while a definition that includes the local unbound environment produces a strong spin bias. Linking stellar surface density to that environmental spin reproduces the observed clustering inside ΛCDM, so the signal is assembly bias rather than new dark-matter physics (Ma, Li, Chen & Mo, accepted by MNRAS).

Figure: stellar surface density versus environmental halo spin in the empirical model.

Two-phase formation of Milky Way-like galaxies

arXiv ADS Publisher

In the FIRE-2 zoom-in simulations, Milky Way-mass galaxies switch from a hot, bulge-dominated, bursty phase to a cold, disk-dominated, steady phase (Ma, Chen & Mo 2026, MNRAS). The switch lines up with the host halo's transition from fast to slow accretion. Once fast accretion stalls and feedback lowers the gas supply, a stellar disk can grow.

Figure: edge-on and face-on stellar maps of the FIRE-2 galaxy m12f at z = 0, split into thin disk, thick disk, and bulge.

Three-stage morphology of massive galaxies

Ma et al. (in prep.)

With the COLIBRE simulations we find a three-stage path for massive galaxies: dispersion-dominated, then disk-dominated, then dispersion-dominated again. We separate the physical processes in each stage, especially while the disk forms and is later transformed.

Dark matter haloes within large-scale structure

How the anisotropic cosmic web affects the evolution of dark matter haloes — how they grow, how they are oriented, and how their satellites are distributed.

Lopsided satellites around paired haloes

Guo, Ma & Li (2025), ApJ — observations

arXiv ADS Publisher

In SDSS, satellites around galaxy-group pairs are lopsided: they prefer the region between the two central galaxies, and they bulge along the line that joins them. Both trends strengthen as the pair separation and halo mass increase. Halo-based mock catalogs reproduce the measurements. Overlapping two mass-matched haloes, once their orientations are aligned with the pair axis, accounts for the signal, so it follows from large-scale structure in ΛCDM.

Ma, Li & Guo (2025), ApJ — theory

arXiv ADS Publisher

In IllustrisTNG, the three-dimensional distribution of subhaloes around halo pairs combines a lopsided excess between the two haloes with a bulging excess along the pair axis. Lopsidedness comes mainly from halo overlap and grows with primary mass. Bulging traces alignment with cosmic filaments and is stronger at larger separation. Projecting from 3D to 2D strongly suppresses the bulging signal.

Figure: schematic of a halo pair and the satellite position angle.

Anisotropic secondary bias of dark matter haloes

arXiv ADS Publisher (submitted to ApJ)

Using TNG300-1-Dark at z = 0, we measure anisotropic secondary bias: how halo clustering depends on properties other than mass, and how that dependence changes with direction relative to the halo major axis (Ma & Li, submitted to ApJ). Orientation-averaged secondary bias is largely removed once tidal anisotropy is matched. The anisotropic signal itself is weak for formation time, concentration, and triaxiality, but strong for spin and shape: slowly rotating and more elongated haloes align more strongly with filaments. Matching the halo–environment alignment removes much of that spin- and shape-dependent signal. Including unbound particles also changes the low-mass spin bias.

Figure: anisotropic secondary bias as a function of halo mass.

A fly-by origin of secondary bias

We construct a chain for how an anisotropic environment modulates halo growth and affects their secondary properties, leading to their secondary bias across the whole mass range and the whole set of secondary properties.

Cosmology

How the redshift evolution of galaxy clustering can constrain cosmology, and how fast simulations can estimate the systematics of that measurement.

Systematics of the tomographic Alcock–Paczynski method

arXiv ADS Publisher

The tomographic Alcock–Paczynski method separates the geometric AP signal from redshift-space distortions and constrains cosmology with clustering on scales ≲ 40 h−1 Mpc (Ma et al. 2020, ApJ). Its main systematic is the redshift evolution of RSD. Comparing BigMultiDark N-body simulations with approximate COLA runs, that evolution is about 5% from z = 0 to 0.5 and about 10% out to z = 1. Errors in the COLA correlation functions are 5–10 times smaller than this intrinsic systematic, so COLA is accurate enough to estimate it. Changing the halo mass from 2×1012 to 1014 M⊙ shifts the statistic by ≲ 1.5%.
