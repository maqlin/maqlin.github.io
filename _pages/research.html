I study how galaxies and dark matter haloes assemble inside the cosmic web. The work falls into two directions.

  1. Dark matter haloes within large-scale structure
  2. Galaxy formation and evolution

Dark matter haloes within large-scale structure

Large-scale structure sets the tidal field in which haloes grow. I use SDSS and cosmological simulations to measure how that environment arranges satellites, and how it imprints halo spin and assembly bias.

Lopsided satellites around paired haloes

arXiv ADS Publisher

Motivated by the Local Group, we measure the angular distribution of satellites around galaxy-group pairs in SDSS (Guo, Ma & Li 2025, ApJ). Satellites prefer the region between the two centrals, and they concentrate along the axis that joins them. A halo-based mock catalog reproduces the signal once the alignment between halo orientation and the pair axis is included, so the pattern follows from structure formation in ΛCDM.

Figure: satellite angle distributions in SDSS and in the mock catalog.

Filaments, overlap, and the 3D satellite distribution

arXiv ADS Publisher

In IllustrisTNG we separate the three-dimensional signal into a lopsided excess between the two haloes and a bulging excess along the pair axis (Ma, Li & Guo 2025, ApJ). Lopsidedness is largely halo overlap. Bulging traces alignment with cosmic filaments. Projecting from 3D to 2D strongly suppresses the bulging signal.

Figure: 3D satellite distribution, P(cos θ), for halo pairs of different separations.

Halo spin bias and ultra-diffuse galaxies

arXiv ADS Publisher (accepted by MNRAS)

Isolated blue ultra-diffuse galaxies in SDSS cluster as strongly as massive groups, which standard subhalo models do not reproduce. In TNG300-Dark the discrepancy depends on the definition of halo spin: spin from bound particles alone fails, while a definition that includes the local unbound environment produces a strong spin bias. Linking stellar surface density to that environmental spin reproduces the observed clustering inside ΛCDM, so the signal is assembly bias rather than new dark-matter physics (Ma, Li, Chen & Mo, accepted by MNRAS).

Figure: stellar surface density versus environmental halo spin in the empirical model.

Galaxy formation and evolution

Galaxy morphology records how baryons settle inside a growing halo. I follow that transition in zoom-in and large-volume hydrodynamic simulations.

Two-phase formation of Milky Way-like galaxies

arXiv ADS Publisher

In the FIRE-2 zoom-in simulations, Milky Way-mass galaxies switch from a hot, bulge-dominated, bursty phase to a cold, disk-dominated, steady phase (Ma, Chen & Mo 2026, MNRAS). The switch lines up with the host halo's transition from fast to slow accretion. Once fast accretion stalls and feedback lowers the gas supply, a stellar disk can grow.

Figure: edge-on and face-on stellar maps of the FIRE-2 galaxy m12f at z = 0, split into thin disk, thick disk, and bulge.

Three-stage morphology of massive galaxies

Ma et al. (in prep.)

With the COLIBRE simulations we find a three-stage path for massive galaxies: dispersion-dominated, then disk-dominated, then dispersion-dominated again. We separate the physical processes in each stage, especially while the disk forms and is later transformed.
